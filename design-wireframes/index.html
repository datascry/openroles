openroles · theme wireframes

Six visual directions for the Filter UI (Phase 4). Each wireframe shows both the mobile (375 px) and desktop (≥ 1024 px) presentations side by side, populated with realistic spec content. The layout, components, and behavior are identical across all six — only the visual theme changes.

Reading order: the editorial family (1, 4, 5, 6) shares typographic discipline and quiet surfaces; the alternates (2, 3) explore very different territory. Direction 1 is the baseline you liked; 4 turns it up, 5 softens it, 6 makes it denser without losing the typographic feel.

Editorial family

typographically driven · low chroma · no card chrome

Direction 1 · baseline Quiet Editorial

Reading, not data

Display serif for titles, generous whitespace, hairline rules instead of borders, single muted ochre accent. Closer to a literary magazine masthead than a feed.

  • Best for evening browsers, not power-scanners
  • Trades density for trust and patience
  • Differentiates strongly from every other job board

Direction 4 · bolder Brutalist Press

Editorial with the volume up

Same typographic discipline as Direction 1 but heavier — display sans for headlines (all caps, weight 800/900), serif body, hot red accent reserved for state changes. Thick rules between sections signal hand-set typography.

  • Strongest visual identity of the six
  • Less precious than Editorial; more confrontational
  • Risk: shouts on small screens; needs careful weight balance
Open wireframe →
Direction 5 · softer Field Guide moss · cream · slab

A Sibley guide for jobs

Warm cream paper, single slab serif throughout, sage-green accent, italicized labels that read like field marks. Each listing is annotated like a species entry with a small marker.

  • Quietest direction — replaces editorial sharpness with naturalist warmth
  • Signals "curated reference," not "feed"
  • Risk: warm palette can read as twee in dark mode
Open wireframe →
Direction 6 · denser Wire Service LIVE · WIRE-RED ACCENT

The morning bulletin

AP / Reuters wire-service aesthetic. Each role is a bulletin: condensed display sans headline, serif lede, dateline showing city and source ATS. Higher density than Quiet Editorial, still typographic — no cards, no shadows.

  • Best density-to-tone ratio of the editorial family
  • Datelines double as ATS attribution — solves a real spec need elegantly
  • Risk: condensed sans needs to be a real font, not a fallback
